# Contrarian Investment, Extrapolation, and Risk

For decades investors had known that value stocks outperform glamour stocks.

However, the reasons why were disputed.

This paper debates that value stocks outperform not because they are inherently more risky and investors need more return, but rather the fact that investors make predictable psychological errors.

This paper looks at data from 1963–1990. It ranks companies by different valuation metrics: book-to-market, cash flow to price, earnings to price, and sales growth.

Then it shorts them and puts the shorted amount into deciles (10 groups), with the companies in group 1 being most expensive and the companies in group 10 being the least expensive.

The paper finds that value companies are actually not riskier than glamour stocks.

## Risk Measures Examined

- Beta (market risk)  
- Return volatility  
- Financial distress  
- Performance in bad economic periods  

Value stocks often have similar or even lower betas than glamour stocks.  
Value firms do not crash more in recessions.  
Their earnings and cash flows are not dramatically riskier.  
Portfolio risk measures do not justify the return difference.

They found that investors almost always make the mistake that they extrapolate sales growth. In reality, the sales growth of glamour stocks almost always falls after a certain period of time back to its historical average.

They also think that many people assume a company is good but overpay for the price.
