# Size Matters if You Control Your Junk

The authors have noted that recent historical evidence shows the size premium is weak.

They first replicated all the classical criticisms of the size effect. Using historical U.S. and global equity data, they confirmed well-known issues such as weak long-term performance, instability over time, concentration in microcaps, January seasonality, and weakness outside the U.S.

They then re-ran different major tests of various size premiums, this time controlling for firm quality.

Stocks were double-sorted by size and quality: small-high quality, small-low quality, large-high quality, and large-low quality.

They showed that all size effect problems vanish once quality is controlled for. A strong and significant size premium emerges, with stability over time, no dependence on microcaps, no January-only effect, strong results across 30 industries and 24 international markets, and robustness to alternative non-price size measurements.

They then validated the results with global robustness tests across international markets, industry-neutral portfolios, alternative size definitions, and liquidity controls.

Finally, they used factor regressions to show that the effect is not explained by other factors. They controlled for market beta, value (HML), momentum (UMD), profitability, investment factors, and illiquidity.
