# Why indexing works

The authors originally wrote this paper because they believed the standard explanations for why active managers underperform were incomplete or incorrect.

They constructed a simple probabilistic model showing that when stock returns are positively skewed, randomly selecting a subset of stocks will tend to produce worse returns than holding the full index.

In their model, stock returns are treated as positively skewed random variables. This assumption is based on previous research showing that a very small fraction of stocks generates the majority of long-term wealth creation.

The index is modeled as the equal-weighted average of all stocks, which simplifies how real market indices actually work but preserves the key intuition.

They then model a situation where an investor randomly selects a smaller subset of stocks to form a portfolio.

Because the average return of the market is higher than the median return, missing even a small number of the biggest winners significantly reduces performance. As a result, the expected return of a randomly selected subset of stocks falls below the return of the index.
