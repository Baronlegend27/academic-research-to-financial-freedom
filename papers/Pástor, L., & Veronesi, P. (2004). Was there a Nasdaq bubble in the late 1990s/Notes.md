
# Was there a Nasdaq bubble in the late 1990s?

This paper develops a formal **valuation model** that incorporates **uncertainty about average future profitability**. The authors use the model to study stock prices, particularly the high valuations of the late 1990s, using Nasdaq data.  

---

## Model Overview

The fundamental value of a stock in this model depends critically on uncertainty about its long-run average profitability. Investors do not know a firm’s true long-run profitability and continuously update their beliefs as new information becomes available. This updating process is formalized using Bayes’ rule, where noisy signals such as earnings data inform investors’ evolving expectations. Profitability itself follows a stochastic process, and the uncertainty surrounding long-run profitability serves as the central state variable in the model. As a result, stock prices reflect not only current profitability but also investors’ evolving beliefs about future profitability.

---

## Key Mechanisms

1. **Belief Updating**
Investors begin with uncertainty about a firm’s long-run profitability and update their beliefs as new earnings reports arrive. This process of belief updating follows Bayes’ rule, and the posterior variance captures the remaining uncertainty after each new signal.

2. **Impact of Uncertainty**
Higher uncertainty has a direct impact on stock prices, increasing both price levels and price volatility. The learning mechanism is particularly important because it allows uncertainty itself to raise valuations, independent of changes in observed profitability.

---

## Model Calibration and Simulation

- The model is **calibrated using Nasdaq data**.
- Parameters are chosen to match **historical observations**.
- Simulations are run to test whether the model can **reproduce historical stock price patterns**, particularly the high prices of the late 1990s.

---

## Analytical Findings

- Uncertainty has the **largest effect on prices when the equity premium is low**.
- Key conclusions:
  - High uncertainty **increases price levels**.
  - Uncertainty **increases price volatility**.
  - The effects of uncertainty **depend on the equity premium**.

---

## Conclusion

The paper shows that modeling **investor beliefs and learning under uncertainty** can explain **historically high stock valuations** and **volatility patterns**, especially during periods of low equity premiums.
