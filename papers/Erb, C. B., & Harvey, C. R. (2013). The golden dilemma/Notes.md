# The Golden Dilemma

This paper examines whether gold is fairly valued and whether it is a good long-term investment.

The authors' first major observation about gold is the fact that it does not produce any cash flows. Unlike firms that have assets, liabilities, and operations, which can be used as anchors for valuations. This makes it more difficult to meaningfully price.

The authors observe the common belief that gold is a perfect inflation hedge. If this is true, then gold will have a very high correlation with inflation. It will also adjust very quickly to changes in inflation. Meaning it has a stable real price over time.

The authors calculate the correlation between gold and inflation, finding that it is not highly correlated.

They also run a regression to see if inflation statistically explains gold's returns, finding that real interest rates, movements in the US dollar, and risk sentiment have more explanatory power in gold's returns. Which makes gold a more speculative investment than most people assume.

In the paper, the authors attempt a few different ways to price gold. Such as the gold-to-inflation relationship, the gold price relative to the US monetary base, and the gold price relative to global GDP. Each of these methods makes very different estimates, illustrating the difficulty of pricing gold.

The authors also analyze the common belief that gold is a safe haven asset. Which is an asset that might not have the best overall returns, however, that performs well during crises, such as hyperinflation and currency collapse.

They authors analyze Brazil, which from 1980 to 2000 had nominal average inflation of 250%. However, during that same time gold had lost 70% of its value. Which is better than the almost 100% loss of the investors who held Brazilian currency or bonds.

This is because gold is priced globally, where the global supply and demand and macroeconomic factors.Gold sometimes kept pace with local hyperinflation, sometimes lagged behind, and at other times exceeded it dramatically.
